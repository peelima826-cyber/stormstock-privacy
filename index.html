Adaptive by design

The app changes as the storm does.

StormStock reads live data from the National Weather Service and the National Hurricane Center, then reshapes itself around what you need at that exact moment.

01 · Calm

Build your kit, check the tropics, find what's nearby. No panic, no noise.

02 · Threat

A watch or the cone reaches you. Supplies and evacuation zones move front and center.

03 · Impact

Tornado warning or landfall. Everything collapses into shelter, SOS, and family.

04 · Recovery

What's open, where the flooding and downed lines are, and documenting damage.

Questions

Before you download.

Is StormStock free?

Yes — completely. No ads, no subscriptions, and you don't need an account. Browsing stores, the map, the weather and reporting stock all work anonymously.

How does it know what's in stock?

Neighbors report what they see at a store — in stock, low, or sold out — and every report is timestamped so you can judge how fresh it is. At gas stations people can also report the current price, and you can sort by cheapest.

Where do the storm alerts come from?

Tornado, severe thunderstorm and flash flood alerts come straight from the National Weather Service, with the official warning area drawn on the map. The hurricane cone and track come from the National Hurricane Center, and open shelters come from FEMA.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet — StormStock is an iPhone and iPad app and needs iOS 15.1 or later. An Android version may follow if enough people ask for it.

Which areas does it cover?

Weather alerts and shelters work anywhere in the United States. The neighbor-reported supply and gas price data is most useful in Central Florida, where the community is biggest — it gets better as more people nearby use it.

Do you sell my data?

No. There are no ads and no trackers, and we don't sell anything to anyone. Your location is used to show what's near you. Read the privacy policy.

What it is

StormStock is a free iPhone app that shows which stores near you still have water, gas, ice, generators and batteries in the days before a hurricane — reported by neighbors rather than pulled from any retailer's inventory.

Someone standing in a store taps an item and marks it in stock, low, or sold out. Every report carries a timestamp, so the next person can judge how current it is. At gas stations, users can also report the price and sort nearby stations from cheapest.

The problem it addresses

In the seventy-two hours before a Florida hurricane, the essentials disappear. People drive from store to store burning the fuel they are trying to find, with no way to know what is left anywhere. Retailers do not publish live inventory, and after the storm the questions get more urgent: which station can still pump, which road is passable, what is open.

Where the data comes from

  • Supply levels and gas prices — reported by users, each with a timestamp.
  • Weather warnings — the National Weather Service, including tornado, storm surge, extreme wind, flash flood and tsunami warnings, with the official warning area drawn on the map and an indication of whether you are inside it.
  • Hurricane track and cone — the National Hurricane Center.
  • Open shelters — FEMA's National Shelter System.
  • Places — OpenStreetMap. Rain radar — RainViewer. Satellite imagery — NASA GIBS.

StormStock is a community tool that displays official data. It is not an official source and does not replace instructions from emergency management.
